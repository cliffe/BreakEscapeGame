# SAFETYNET Field Guide: Lockpicking

## Objective
Use this guide when you reach a pin-tumbler lock — an office door, cabinet, or storage container — and cooperation or a key is not available. The goal is to read the lock by feel and set its pins one at a time, not to force it.

## Quick Reference
- Lockpicking is diagnostic, not brute force. Apply light tension and respond to what the lock tells you.
- Two tools work together: a **tension wrench** and a **pick**.
- Tension is the critical variable. Too much jams the pins; too little drops the ones you have set.
- Work the **binding pin** (the stiff one), set it, then move to the next.

| Tool | Role |
|------|------|
| Tension wrench | Light rotational pressure in the keyway, in the direction the key would turn |
| Pick | Probes, lifts, and sets individual pins above the wrench |

## How Pin Tumbler Locks Work
Inside the cylinder are spring-loaded pin stacks, each with a key pin and a driver pin. With no key, the driver pins cross the **shear line** (the gap between the rotating plug and the fixed housing), blocking rotation. The correct key lifts every stack so each boundary sits exactly at the shear line, freeing the plug.

Picking exploits manufacturing tolerance: under rotational pressure, one pin binds before the others. Set that binding pin at the shear line, hold it with friction, and repeat.

## Core Workflow
1. **Apply tension.** Insert the wrench at the bottom of the keyway. Apply light, steady pressure in the key's turning direction and maintain it throughout.
2. **Find the binding pin.** Probe with the pick. Most pins feel springy; the binding pin feels stiffer and heavier. That is your target.
3. **Set it.** Lift the binding pin gently until you feel a faint click and a tiny plug rotation. Do not release tension.
4. **Move to the next.** A new pin now binds. Probe, lift, and set it the same way.
5. **Repeat until open.** When the last pin reaches the shear line, the plug turns freely.

## Common Failure Modes
- **Pins won't set:** too much tension. Ease off until the minimum that holds.
- **Set pins keep dropping:** too little tension, or you released it. Re-apply light, steady pressure.
- **Plug rotates slightly then stalls (false set):** likely a security pin (spool/serrated). Hold tension and keep probing — real sets stay; security pins need a small extra nudge over the step.
- **Progress is slow:** rushing resets pins. A methodical pass is faster than restarting.

## In the Field
> **[GAMEPLAY NOTE]** This describes how the technique is simulated in your environment; the principle is identical. Engage the tension wrench (the plug shows a small rotation), focus on the highlighted binding pin, lift it until it sets, then repeat as the highlight moves to the next pin. Disengaging tension drops set pins. Harder locks have more pins and may include security pins, which demand careful tension management through the false set.

## Mission Application
Several rooms and containers here are pin-tumbler locked. Trying to talk your way in or earn a key is usually cleaner, but when neither is on the table, your kit will get you through. Stay quiet, work the binding pin, and keep tension light and steady.
